<taskflow>
    <subtask name="Liability_Coverages">
        <step name="Bodily_Injury_and_Property_Damage">
            <trigger>User asks what Bodily Injury (BI) or Property Damage (PD) covers, or why they need it.</trigger>
            <action>
                - Bodily Injury (BI): Covers costs if you injure someone else in an accident — including their medical bills, lost wages, and legal fees if they sue you. It is required in most states and protects your personal assets.
                - Property Damage (PD): Pays for damage you cause to someone else's vehicle or property (e.g., a fence, building, or parked car) in an at-fault accident.
            </action>
        </step>

        <step name="Split_Limits_100_300">
            <trigger>User asks about Bodily Injury limits like 100/300 or how much coverage they need.</trigger>
            <action>
                - 100/300 Limits: The first number ($100,000) is the maximum paid per person injured; the second ($300,000) is the maximum paid per accident regardless of how many people are injured.
                - Recommended Limits: A common recommendation is to carry limits at least equal to your net worth to protect savings and assets in a serious accident.
            </action>
        </step>

        <step name="UM_and_UIM_Coverage">
            <trigger>User asks about Uninsured Motorist (UM) or Underinsured Motorist (UIM) coverage, differences, or rejection rules.</trigger>
            <action>
                - Uninsured Motorist (UM): Pays for your injuries and damages if you are hit by a driver with no insurance. Required in many states.
                - Underinsured Motorist (UIM): Kicks in when the at-fault driver has insurance, but their limits cannot cover your full losses.
                - Difference & Both: UM covers zero insurance; UIM fills the gap. Carrying both provides complete protection.
                - Rejection Rules: In some states you may reject UM/UIM in writing, but in states such as Nebraska, South Dakota, New Hampshire, and Minnesota, rejection is not permitted.
            </action>
        </step>
    </subtask>

    <subtask name="Physical_Damage_and_Deductibles">
        <step name="Comprehensive_vs_Collision">
            <trigger>User asks about Comprehensive coverage, Collision coverage, or the difference between them.</trigger>
            <action>
                - Comprehensive: Covers damage to your vehicle from events other than a collision — such as theft, vandalism, fire, hail, flooding, or hitting an animal.
                - Collision: Covers damage to your vehicle when it collides with another vehicle or object, regardless of fault.
                - Difference: Comprehensive covers non-collision perils; Collision covers impact. Lenders usually require both for loans or leases.
            </action>
        </step>

        <step name="Deductible_Selection_and_Rate_Impact">
            <trigger>User asks how to choose between a $250, $500, or $1,000 deductible, or how lowering a deductible affects premium.</trigger>
            <action>
                - Choosing a Deductible: A higher deductible lowers your premium but requires more out of pocket when filing a claim. If you have emergency savings, a higher deductible is a smart trade-off. If cash flow is tight, a lower deductible provides predictability.
                - Premium Impact: Rate impact depends on vehicle, location, and driving history. After making changes, click the "Calculate new rate" button to see the updated premium in real time.
            </action>
        </step>
    </subtask>

    <subtask name="Optional_and_Addon_Coverages">
        <step name="Rental_and_Roadside">
            <trigger>User asks about Rental Reimbursement or Emergency Roadside Assistance (ERA).</trigger>
            <action>
                - Rental Reimbursement: Pays for a rental car while your vehicle is repaired after a covered claim. Worth adding if you rely on your vehicle daily.
                - Emergency Roadside Assistance (ERA): Covers towing, flat tire changes, battery jump-starts, and lockout assistance. Costco Executive members may have this defaulted to "Included".
            </action>
        </step>

        <step name="OEM_and_Gap_Coverage">
            <trigger>User asks about OEM Parts coverage or Loan/Lease Assistance (Gap).</trigger>
            <action>
                - OEM Parts: Ensures repairs use factory-original parts rather than aftermarket alternatives. Available for vehicles generally up to 11 model years old and requires Comprehensive or Collision.
                - Loan/Lease Assistance (Gap): Covers the gap between the car's depreciated market value payout and what you still owe on your loan or lease if totaled. Requires both Comprehensive and Collision.
            </action>
        </step>

        <step name="New_Car_Road_Trip_and_ADD">
            <trigger>User asks about Road Trip Accident Accommodations, New Car Replacement, or AD&D.</trigger>
            <action>
                - Road Trip Accommodations: Pays for lodging, meals, and transportation if in an accident over a set distance from home with a disabled vehicle.
                - New Car Replacement: Replaces a totaled brand-new vehicle with a new vehicle of same make/model rather than depreciated value. Requires Comp and Collision.
                - Accidental Death & Dismemberment (AD&D): Provides a benefit for death or loss of limb resulting from a covered auto accident.
            </action>
        </step>

        <step name="MedPay_vs_PIP">
            <trigger>User asks about Medical Expense (MedPay) or Personal Injury Protection (PIP).</trigger>
            <action>
                - Medical Expense (MedPay): Covers medical bills for you and your passengers after an accident regardless of fault (co-pays, deductibles, immediate treatment).
                - Personal Injury Protection (PIP): Broader no-fault coverage required in certain states covering medical expenses, lost wages, and funeral costs. In states where PIP is available, it generally cannot be combined with Medical Expense coverage.
            </action>
        </step>
    </subtask>
